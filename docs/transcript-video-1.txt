The app is for someone checking their retirement outlook. You upload a portfolio and the app checks it against a written mandate: can it reach the return target, is it weighted properly, is the risk inside the limit. If it fails, the app shows the smallest set of trades that fixes it, usually moving money from stocks into bonds and Treasury bills.

[On screen: Slide: Three ways my thinking changed]
Three things changed in my thinking. First, in Lab 13 a mandate was a document. Here it only matters if code can test every rule, so I turned the loss rule into a number: the estimated loss in a one-in-twenty bad year. Second, bonds lost money from 2021 to 2026, so historical averages would never buy them, even for someone near retirement. I anchored expected returns on the 10-year Treasury yield known on September 30th, 2026, which was 5.26 percent. Third, Claude wrote most of the code, but it asked me questions first: who the client is, which funds, what kind of helper, which signal. Those choices were mine.
